# Is It Legal to Listen to British ATC Using a Radio Scanner? (UK)

## Summary

**No — it is illegal in the UK** to use a radio scanner to listen to Air Traffic Control communications, even in receive-only mode.

## Legal Basis

### Wireless Telegraphy Act 2006, Section 48

- **Section 48(1)**: It is an offence to use wireless telegraphy apparatus with intent to obtain information about the contents, sender, or addressee of any message that the person is not authorised to receive.
- **Section 48(2)**: It is an offence to disclose information obtained this way.
- Penalties can include fines and up to **2 years imprisonment** on conviction on indictment.

### Key Points

- There is **no general exemption** for aviation enthusiasts or for "receive-only" use.
- Simply owning a scanner capable of receiving these frequencies is not itself illegal, but **using it to listen to ATC is**.
- The law does **not** distinguish between "just listening" and recording or redistributing — both are offences.

## Comparison with Other Countries

| Country     | Legal to Listen to ATC? |
|-------------|------------------------|
| **UK**      | No (Wireless Telegraphy Act 2006, s.48) |
| **USA**     | Yes (legal and common) |
| **Canada**  | Yes (receive-only is legal) |
| **Australia** | No (similar restrictions to UK) |

## Legal Alternatives

- **LiveATC.net** — Many UK ATC feeds are streamed online. Listening to internet streams is not covered by the Wireless Telegraphy Act.
- **Ofcom-licensed services** — Some authorised broadcasts are legal to receive.

## Disclaimer

This is general legal information, not legal advice. For specific situations, consult a qualified solicitor. Enforcement against individuals listening at home has historically been rare, but the activity remains technically illegal.
